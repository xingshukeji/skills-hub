# Reusable Course Critique Patterns

Use these as decision constraints, not as prose to paste into lessons.

## Repeated User Objections

1. **The course is too shallow after the basics.** Later lessons must add mechanism, numbers, failure modes, comparison conditions, or research evidence. Rephrased common sense is not depth.
2. **A textbook must not mislead.** Definitions, formulas, metrics, current model claims, and diagrams need explicit scope. A visually polished false simplification is worse than no diagram.
3. **Prefer diagrams where relationships are spatial or sequential.** Formulas and algorithms can often be shown as nodes, planes, vectors, layers, and connecting lines, but the picture must preserve the mathematical operation and dimensions.
4. **Benchmark content is a live reference surface.** Show real leaderboards, sources, dates, metric direction, uncertainty, and scenario-specific selection. Do not turn it into a generic terminology course.
5. **Do not force one template onto every content type.** Lessons, maps, leaderboards, indexes, glossaries, interactive tools, and paper libraries have different jobs.
6. **Information density matters.** Prefer charts, comparison tables, exact terms, and direct evidence over repeated introductions and motivational prose.
7. **Two strong pages can be better than seven weak ones.** Split only when each page owns an independent learner decision or interaction.
8. **Support first-time and repeat reading simultaneously.** Beginners need a coherent causal path. Experienced readers need bold judgments, numbered summaries, headings, numbers, and tables that reveal what can be skipped.
9. **Do not explain the interface through more prose.** Encode importance and optionality in the page hierarchy.
10. **Delete obsolete pages when compatibility is not required.** Do not preserve redirects, aliases, or migration notices by default.

## Red Flags

- Multiple pages answer “where should I start?” with nearly identical lists.
- Every page begins with the same rationale, goals, terms, and acceptance template regardless of content type.
- A paragraph repeats its heading, diagram, and table without adding evidence.
- A specialist chapter lists benefits but omits actual computation, tradeoffs, or failure modes.
- A leaderboard assigns a custom credibility grade without explaining its evidence basis.
- A short page exists only because a filename or old route once existed.
- A long page cannot be reconstructed from its headings and emphasized claims.

## Preserve Despite Low Character Count

- A page hosting a distinct interactive control or browser-local progress state.
- A compact glossary, formula sheet, checklist, or current leaderboard used repeatedly.
- A stable source record or route explicitly required by the product.
- A support/legal/privacy surface with a separate responsibility.
